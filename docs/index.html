Why Rigsight

Most monitors forget.
Rigsight remembers.

A normal hardware monitor

  • Shows numbers only while you're watching
  • Close it, and the numbers are gone
  • A wall of sensors, no context
  • "Did it overheat last night?" — no idea

Rigsight

  • Records all day, even with its window closed
  • Any day, week or month, minute by minute
  • Every number tied to the app that was in front
  • "GPU hit 74° at 8:14 PM in Cyberpunk" — yes
Temperatures

Every degree, with a timestamp.

Live CPU and GPU gauges, hot spot and memory junction, per-core load, fans, clocks, power and voltages. Each shows today's low and high, recorded even when the window is closed.

  • History for the last 5 minutes to 24 hours, or any single day
  • Hover the chart for the exact time and temperature
  • Alerts that ignore one-second spikes and only fire when it stays hot
Reports

Your day, minute by minute.

Pick any day, week or month. A timeline shows which app was in front each minute, with CPU and GPU temperature drawn over it, plus your peaks and when they happened.

  • Active time vs. time the PC was just on
  • Highlights that only speak up when something stands out
  • Idle vs. idle and load vs. load comparisons with last week
Apps

Where your time and heat go.

Every app and game, measured: time you actually used it, time it sat in the background, and time it was minimized. See how hot each one runs your CPU and GPU, and how much memory it takes.

  • Any day, the last 7 or 30 days, or all time
  • A fullscreen game counts as in use even on a controller
  • Rename apps, set categories, or never track one
Crashes

Crashes, explained in plain words.

Game crashes, freezes, graphics driver resets, blue screens and sudden shutdowns, read from Windows' own logs and translated into what probably happened and what to try.

  • The temperatures and game right before each crash
  • "What changed before": drivers and updates installed in the days before
  • Patterns, one-click reports to paste in a forum, and mute for noisy apps
In-game overlay

Your numbers, inside the game.

Press Alt Shift O in any game. FPS, 1% lows, frame time, CPU and GPU temperature, load, clock and power, VRAM, RAM, and up to 10 of your own sensors.

  • Works in exclusive fullscreen, through RivaTuner (installed for you)
  • Fade the panel and the numbers separately, down to just the numbers
  • Never takes focus; clicks pass straight through
  • Rigsight never hooks into games, so it's safe with anti-cheat

Get started

Running in two minutes.

One command

Open PowerShell and paste this. It downloads the latest setup from GitHub, checks it against GitHub's checksum and opens it. Run it again any time to update.

irm https://b0llu.github.io/Rigsight/install.ps1 | iex

or download it

  1. 1

    Download

    Grab the installer. It's free, with no account and no ads.

  2. 2

    Install

    Accept the one admin prompt. It sets up the recorder to start with Windows, plus the free sensor driver and RivaTuner if you want them.

  3. 3

    Forget about it

    Use your PC as usual. Open Rigsight whenever you're curious; the history is already there.

Downloaded in a browser, Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the installer isn't code-signed yet: click More info → Run anyway, or use the PowerShell command above, which doesn't trigger it. The full source is on GitHub if you'd like to check it first.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Rigsight is free and open source under the MIT license. No trial, no premium tier, no ads.

Does it work with AMD and Intel?

Yes. Hardware is read through LibreHardwareMonitor, which supports NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics cards and Intel and AMD processors. NVIDIA cards also get an extra-light fast path.

Will the overlay get me banned?

Rigsight never hooks into games. Over borderless and windowed games its overlay is an ordinary always-on-top window. For exclusive fullscreen it uses RivaTuner Statistics Server, the long-established tool behind MSI Afterburner's overlay, which anti-cheat systems accept.

Will it slow my games down?

No. The background agent uses around 0.006% of your CPU with the window closed, runs at below-normal priority, and reads only what the tray and widgets need.

What does it record, exactly?

Which app is in front and for how long, and how hard your hardware is working: temperatures, loads, clocks, power, fans, memory and drives. It never records window titles, keystrokes, screenshots or file contents, and nothing is sent anywhere.

How much history does it keep?

As much as you like, from three months to forever. Temperatures, app time, sessions and crashes all go back equally far, so any day you look back on is complete. A year of history takes about 25 MB.

How do I uninstall it?

Settings → Apps → Rigsight → Uninstall. That removes the program and its startup task. Your history stays in %LocalAppData%\Rigsight until you delete the folder.
