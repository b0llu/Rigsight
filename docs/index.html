Why Rigsight

Most monitors forget.
Rigsight remembers.

Pick any day and it's all still there: what was running, how hot it got, and the minute it happened.

A normal hardware monitor

  • Shows numbers only while you're watching
  • Close it, and the numbers are gone
  • "Did it overheat last night?" — no idea

Rigsight

  • Records all day, even with its window closed
  • Any day, week, month or year, minute by minute
  • "GPU hit 70° at 1:59 PM in Counter-Strike 2" — yes

What happened

Look back at any day.

Temperatures

Every degree, with a timestamp.

Live CPU and GPU gauges, hot spot and memory junction, per-core load, fans, clocks, power and voltages. Each shows today's low and high, recorded even when the window is closed.

  • History from the last 5 minutes to any day, week, month or year
  • Hover the chart for the time, with the average, highest and lowest
  • Alerts that ignore one-second spikes and only fire when it stays hot
Reports

Any day, week, month or year.

A day shows which app was in front each minute, with CPU and GPU temperature drawn over it. A week, a month or a year shows where the time went, with your peaks and when they happened.

  • Active time vs. time the PC was just on
  • Highlights that only speak up when something stands out: who made the heat, records, a fan turning slower than it used to
  • Temperatures compared with your usual, idle with idle and load with load, this week and months ago

When something goes wrong

Find out why.

Crashes

Crashes, explained in plain words.

Game crashes, freezes, graphics driver resets, blue screens and sudden shutdowns, read from Windows' own logs and translated into what probably happened and what to try.

  • The temperatures and game right before each crash
  • "What changed before": drivers and updates installed in the days before
  • Patterns, one-click reports to paste in a forum, and mute for noisy apps
Timeline

What changed on your PC, day by day.

Drivers and Windows updates, apps installed, updated or removed, what starts with Windows, new hardware, the BIOS and the settings that matter. Each one on the day it happened, down to a switch of power plan.

  • After a driver or an update: whether problems went up or down since
  • A calendar to jump to any day, with problem days marked
  • By day, or a whole month in one row
RiggyExperimental

Or just ask.

Riggy is a helper in the corner of the window. Ask in your own words why a game crashed, how hot it runs, what changed this week or where your time went, and it answers from your PC's own records.

  • Runs on your PC. Nothing you type leaves it
  • Every number is read from what Rigsight recorded, never guessed
  • Still learning: tell it what you meant and it remembers your wording
  • Not for you? Turn it off in Settings

Part by part

Every part of your PC, on its own page.

Apps

Where your time and heat go.

Every app and game, measured: time you actually used it, time it sat in the background, and time it was minimized. See how hot each one runs your CPU and GPU, and how much memory it takes.

  • Any day, week, month or year, or all time
  • A fullscreen game counts as in use even on a controller
  • Rename apps, set categories, or never track one
Network

Where your internet goes.

How much you downloaded and uploaded, and which app used it. Each app's use is split into what you were using, what ran in the background, and what downloaded while you were away.

  • Any day, week, month or year, with your biggest downloads
  • Spots apps that quietly use your internet in the background
  • Notices when your connection drops, or downloads get slower than usual
Fans

Every fan, and what wakes it up.

Every fan on your graphics card and motherboard. See when your GPU fans start spinning, how long they stood still, how fast they turn at each temperature, and which game made them work hardest.

  • Any day, week, month or year
  • A quick check that every fan is OK
  • A heads-up when a fan stops or turns slower than it used to
Memory

What's filling your RAM.

How much memory is in use, free and compressed, and which apps are using it right now. Open an app to see each of its processes: every browser tab, every Windows service.

  • The heaviest apps stand out at a glance
  • Today's biggest memory users, with their peaks
Storage

How full, and how healthy.

How full each drive is and how fast it's filling. SSD wear, SMART health and bad sectors, and a map of what's taking the space.

  • Space you could free: caches, temporary files, old downloads
  • See what's inside any drive or folder
  • Nothing is deleted for you: it shows where the space is, and you decide

Without opening the window

Your numbers, where you want them.

In-game overlay

Your numbers, inside the game.

Press Alt Shift O in any game. FPS, 1% lows, frame time, CPU and GPU temperature, load, clock and power, VRAM, RAM, and up to 10 of your own sensors.

  • Works in exclusive fullscreen, through RivaTuner (installed for you)
  • Colour or grayscale; fade the panel and the numbers separately
  • Never takes focus; clicks pass straight through
  • Rigsight never hooks into games, so it's safe with anti-cheat
Desktop widgets

Small readouts on your desktop.

Seven ready-made widgets, from FPS to a temperature graph, or make your own: a bar, tiles, gauges or a graph with any readings you like.

  • Edit any of them, in dark, grey or light, colour or grayscale, in sizes up to 3XL
  • Drag one anywhere, or lock it so clicks pass straight through
  • They step aside while you play
Taskbar

Readings beside the clock.

Put temperatures, load and power right in the taskbar, next to the clock, where they are always in view.

  • In the taskbar itself, an icon for each reading, or an icon per part
  • A colour for each part, or grayscale
  • Pick any readings and put them in your order

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Rigsight is free and open source under the MIT license. No trial, no premium tier, no ads.

Does it work with AMD and Intel?

Yes. Hardware is read through LibreHardwareMonitor, which supports NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics cards and Intel and AMD processors. NVIDIA cards also get an extra-light fast path.

Will the overlay get me banned?

Rigsight never hooks into games. Over borderless and windowed games its overlay is an ordinary always-on-top window. For exclusive fullscreen it uses RivaTuner Statistics Server, the long-established tool behind MSI Afterburner's overlay, which anti-cheat systems accept.

Will it slow my games down?

No. In the background Rigsight uses around 0.02% of your CPU and runs at low priority, so your games always come first.

What does it record, exactly?

Which app is in front and for how long, and how hard your hardware is working: temperatures, loads, clocks, power, fans, memory and drives. It also counts how much each app downloads and uploads. It never records window titles, keystrokes, screenshots, file contents or the websites you visit, and none of it is sent anywhere.

What is Riggy?

A helper in the corner of the window that answers questions about your PC, such as why a game crashed or how hot it ran, from what Rigsight has recorded. It runs entirely on your PC, so nothing you type is sent anywhere. It is experimental and still learning, and you can turn it off in Settings.

How do I update it?

You don't have to. New versions download quietly in the background and install the next time you start your PC. Want it sooner? Click Restart in the app and you're on the new version in seconds.

How much history does it keep?

As much as you like, from three months to forever. Temperatures, app time, sessions and crashes all go back equally far, so any day you look back on is complete. A year of history takes about 35 MB.

How do I uninstall it?

Settings → Apps → Rigsight → Uninstall. That removes the program and its startup task. Your history stays in %LocalAppData%\Rigsight until you delete the folder.
