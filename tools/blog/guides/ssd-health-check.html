SSDs don't click or grind before they fail, so the only way to know how yours is doing is to ask it. Every SSD keeps a record of its own health, called SMART data, and Windows 11 can read some of it without installing anything. This SSD health check for Windows 11 takes a few minutes. It covers Windows Settings, PowerShell and free SSD health check software.

Check SSD health in Windows 11 Settings

Windows has a drive health page built in. It's the quickest check, but Microsoft says Windows only monitors NVMe SSDs here, which most small stick shaped drives in an M.2 slot are. SATA SSDs, including M.2 SATA ones, and hard drives don't show health here.

  1. Open Settings with Windows+I.
  2. Go to System > Storage.
  3. Under Storage management, open Advanced storage settings > Disks & volumes.
  4. Pick your SSD and select Properties.
  5. Look at the Drive health section.

You'll see three readings.

  • Estimated remaining life is how much of its rated write life the SSD thinks it has left.
  • Available spare is the spare memory the drive keeps to replace cells that wear out.
  • Temperature is the drive's temperature right now.

If the drive reports a problem, Windows shows a warning such as "Reliability is degraded" and tells you to back up your data. Windows 10 has the same page under Settings > System > Storage > Manage disks and volumes, then Properties.

Check SSD health with PowerShell

PowerShell reads any drive, SATA or NVMe, and gives a few more numbers. The old wmic diskdrive get status trick no longer works, because Microsoft has removed WMIC from current Windows 11.

  1. Right click the Start button and choose Terminal (Admin).
  2. For a quick yes or no, run this command. 
    Get-PhysicalDisk | Select-Object FriendlyName, MediaType, HealthStatus, OperationalStatus
  3. For wear and error counts, run this one. 
    Get-PhysicalDisk | Get-StorageReliabilityCounter | Select-Object DeviceId, Wear, Temperature, PowerOnHours, ReadErrorsUncorrected, WriteErrorsUncorrected

HealthStatus should say Healthy. Warning or Unhealthy means back up now. In the second command, Wear is the percentage of the drive's rated wear already used, so it counts up. A new drive reads 0, and Microsoft describes 100 as the point where the estimated wear limit is reached. Some drives leave Wear empty. Uncorrected read or write errors should be 0. Anything above that, and growing, is a warning sign.

Free SSD health check software

A SMART tool shows everything the drive reports, in one window. Two kinds are worth having.

CrystalDiskInfo

CrystalDiskInfo is free, small and reads almost any drive. Install it, open it, and each drive gets a box at the top marked Good, Caution or Bad, with a percentage for SSDs. Below that is the full SMART table, power on hours and the total data written.

Your SSD maker's tool

Most SSD makers have a free tool for their own drives. Examples are Samsung Magician, Sandisk Dashboard for WD and Sandisk drives, which used to be called WD Dashboard, Crucial Storage Executive, Kingston SSD Manager and others. They show health in the maker's own terms, and they can update the SSD's firmware, which sometimes fixes bugs that affect speed or reliability. Back up before a firmware update, and only use the maker's tool for it.

What the readings mean

The names change between tools, but they come down to a few things.

  • Life left, health or percentage used. SSDs wear a little with every write, and the maker rates how much they can take. 90% left means it has used about a tenth of that. Most home PCs write far less than the rating, so this number usually drops slowly over years.
  • Total bytes written, or TBW. The data written so far. Compare it with the TBW rating on the drive's spec sheet.
  • Available spare. When it falls under the drive's threshold, the drive reports a warning.
  • Media errors, reallocated or pending sectors, and uncorrectable errors. These should be 0. A few that never change aren't an emergency. A count that keeps growing is the clearest sign a drive is on its way out.
  • Temperature. NVMe SSDs slow themselves down when they get too hot, often somewhere in the 70s or 80s °C, depending on the model. If yours runs that hot in games, a heatsink on the M.2 slot helps.

Two things to keep in mind. The life figure is an estimate, and a drive at 0% doesn't die that day. And a drive marked healthy can still fail without warning, from a controller fault for example. A health check makes a surprise less likely, but only a backup protects your files.

What to do if your SSD isn't healthy

  1. Back up what matters, now, to another drive or a cloud service.
  2. Check the maker's tool for a firmware update.
  3. Check the warranty. Many SSD warranties cover a set number of years or a TBW figure, whichever comes first.
  4. Plan to replace the drive, and check it again every week until you do.

If the drive is just full rather than worn, that's a different problem. What is taking up space on your C drive helps with that.

How RigSight keeps an eye on it

RigSight is a free, open source app that records your PC all day and keeps it as history. Its Storage page has a Drive health card that lists every drive with one word for its state, which is Healthy, Early warning or Failing. Under each name it shows the life left the SSD reports and the hours it has been on. Drives that report bad sectors, usually SATA ones, also show the count, or "No bad sectors".

When a drive isn't healthy, RigSight says why on its card, in plain words. "Bad sectors are an early warning. Keep a backup, and watch whether the count grows." An SSD that reports 10% or less of its rated life left is marked Early warning, even when SMART says it's fine. The Home page's PC health card includes the drives, so you see a problem without going looking for it.

Drive health needs RigSight to run with admin rights. On a standard Windows account, the card says so, and Storage still shows how full each drive is and how fast it's filling.

Run the Settings check or the first PowerShell command today, and write down the life left. Checking it again in a few months tells you more than one reading ever can.
