Search for free PC monitoring software and you get the same few names every time. They aren't really rivals. Each one answers a different question about your PC, and the best choice depends on which question you have. Here is what each free tool is good at, where it falls short, and which HWiNFO or MSI Afterburner alternative fits what you want to know. This is about watching your own hardware, not about tracking other people's computers.

Start with the question

  • Is my CPU or GPU too hot right now, and what is every sensor saying? Use HWiNFO.
  • Can I overclock or undervolt my graphics card, set a fan curve and see FPS in game? Use MSI Afterburner with RivaTuner Statistics Server.
  • Do I want a small open source sensor reader? Use LibreHardwareMonitor.
  • What did my PC do last night, last week or before it crashed? Use RigSight.
  • Which app is eating CPU or memory right now? Task Manager is already on your PC.

Task Manager

Built into Windows, nothing to install. Press Ctrl + Shift + Esc and open Performance for graphs of CPU, memory, disk, network and GPU use. On many PCs with a dedicated graphics card it also shows the GPU temperature, as long as the driver supports it. It doesn't show CPU temperature, fan speeds or voltages, and its graphs only cover the last minute. Good for finding which app is busy, not for checking temperatures.

HWiNFO

HWiNFO reads more sensors than any other tool here. It reads nearly every sensor on the CPU, graphics card, motherboard and drives, down to individual voltages, power limits and memory timings, and it is updated often for new hardware. It can log every sensor to a CSV file, raise alerts on any reading, show readings in the tray and on screen, and share its sensor data with other apps.

It is free for personal and non-commercial use. Commercial use needs the paid Pro licence. In the free version, the shared memory feature that lets other apps such as overlays read HWiNFO's sensors switches itself off after 12 hours, and you turn it on again by hand. It isn't open source.

Where it falls short is the sensor window, which is a long list of numbers, and it doesn't tell you which ones matter or what is normal for your PC. It only logs while you have logging switched on, and you read the CSV files yourself. If you are diagnosing a fault and know what you are looking for, nothing beats it.

MSI Afterburner and RivaTuner Statistics Server

MSI Afterburner is a free graphics card tool that works with cards from other makers too, not only MSI. It is the usual choice for overclocking and undervolting a GPU, setting your own fan curve and watching GPU readings on a graph. Its in-game overlay comes from RivaTuner Statistics Server, a separate free tool that installs alongside it. RivaTuner also has a frame rate limiter that many players use on its own.

Its weak spot is that it is built around the graphics card. CPU and motherboard readings are thinner than HWiNFO's, and the graphs show the recent past while the app is open. Unless you turn on its log file, it keeps no record of how a game ran last week, and it doesn't tell you what the numbers mean. MSI recommends downloading it only from msi.com or Guru3D, because fake copies exist.

If you only want an MSI Afterburner alternative to show FPS, you may not need Afterburner at all. RivaTuner can show a frame rate counter by itself. NVIDIA's and AMD's own apps have performance overlays, and the Xbox Game Bar, opened with Win + G, has a Performance widget with FPS.

LibreHardwareMonitor

LibreHardwareMonitor is free and open source, under the MPL 2.0 licence. It reads temperatures, fan speeds, voltages, load and clocks from motherboards, Intel and AMD processors, NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics cards, drives and network cards. Some sensors need it to run as administrator. Its library is also used inside other apps, RigSight among them.

Its weak spots are that it reads fewer sensors than HWiNFO, and it shows what is happening now unless you turn on Log Sensors, which saves the readings to a file. Get it from the project's GitHub page. The project warns that librehardwaremonitor.com is not its site.

Where RigSight fits

RigSight is a free and open source PC monitor built around one thing the others don't do. It records all day in the background and keeps it. You can open the Temperatures page and look at your CPU and GPU over the last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or 12 months, with the app that made the heat. The Crashes page lists app crashes, blue screens, driver resets and sudden shut-offs with what most likely caused each one. The Timeline lists what changed on the PC, such as drivers and Windows updates, and whether things ran hotter or less stable afterwards.

It also keeps time per app, internet use per app, fan speeds over months, and each game's frame rate and 1% low for every play. Live readings can go in the taskbar, in desktop widgets and in an in-game overlay. Everything can be exported as CSV.

It falls short in a few places. It reads its sensors with LibreHardwareMonitor's library, so it shows fewer sensors than HWiNFO. It can't overclock, undervolt or control fans. Afterburner and your motherboard's software do that. FPS readings and the overlay in exclusive full screen games need RivaTuner Statistics Server. CPU temperature and power, motherboard fans, drive health and per-app internet use need RigSight to run with admin rights. It runs on Windows 10 and 11 only.

A common setup is RigSight running all the time for the history, with HWiNFO or Afterburner opened when you need a closer look or want to tune something.
