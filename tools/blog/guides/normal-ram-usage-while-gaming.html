My PC has 16 GB of memory. While I played Phantom Blade Zero, the game itself used about 4 GB on average and 6.8 GB at its highest. The whole PC meanwhile sat at 13.8 GB on average and reached 15.6 GB. So the game was well under half of what Task Manager showed. The rest was Windows and the apps I keep open. That gap is the first thing to understand about normal RAM usage while gaming.

How RigSight caught it

RigSight records memory every minute twice, once for the whole PC and once for each app. That's what splits the 13.8 GB into the game's own 4 GB and everything else. The Memory page lists each app's memory next to its usual, so an app holding more than it normally does stands out.

It also watches Windows' committed memory, which is what apps have asked for, against the limit of memory plus the page file. During Phantom Blade Zero mine averaged 94% of that limit. When stutters bunch up, five or more within 15 minutes, while committed memory is at 90% or more, RigSight names that as what was going on, "while Windows was nearly out of memory". Since 1.7.0 it can also tell you when an app keeps growing in memory for hours, under Apps that keep growing in memory in Settings, off at first. RigSight is free and open source.

How much RAM do games use?

Here's each game's own memory on my PC, and the whole PC's at the same time.

  • Slay the Spire 2 used about 1 GB, with the PC at 9.5 GB.
  • Risk of Rain 2 used about 2 GB, up to 4.9, with the PC at 11 GB.
  • Dota 2 used about 2.9 GB, with the PC at 12.9 GB.
  • Phantom Blade Zero used about 4.1 GB, up to 6.8, with the PC at 13.8 GB.
  • Crimson Desert used about 4.6 GB, up to 5.8, with the PC at 14.5 GB.

With no game running, my PC sits around 9 GB. Big open-world and new games can use 8 GB or more of their own, and some recommend 32 GB on their store pages.

Normal RAM usage while gaming, in percent

60 to 90% is common, and on a 16 GB PC with a browser open, 80 to 90% is ordinary. High usage alone isn't a problem. Windows keeps recently used files in spare memory to load them faster, and gives that memory back the moment a game needs it.

It becomes a problem when there's nothing left to give back. Then Windows moves data to the page file on the drive, which is far slower, and you feel it.

  • Stutters or long hitches, especially when you turn into a new area.
  • Alt-tabbing out takes seconds, and the game is slow to come back.
  • A Windows warning that the PC is low on memory, or a game closing on its own.

How to check RAM usage while gaming

  1. Open Task Manager with Ctrl + Shift + Esc and go to Performance > Memory.
  2. In use is the PC's total. Committed shows two numbers, what apps have asked for and the limit. When the first gets close to the second, Windows is nearly out of memory.
  3. On the Processes tab, sort by Memory to see the game's own share and what else is holding memory.

How to lower RAM usage while gaming

  1. Close browsers and apps you don't need while you play. Browsers with many tabs or video are often the biggest after the game.
  2. Turn off apps that start with Windows under Settings > Apps > Startup.
  3. Restart the PC before a long session, which clears apps that grew over the day.
  4. Leave the page file on, managed by Windows. Turning it off makes running out of memory worse, not better.
  5. Lower texture quality if the game also fills the GPU's memory, since some spills into the PC's memory.
  6. If you're always near full, more memory is the real fix. Going from 16 to 32 GB is the usual step for gaming now.

Your next step is to check Committed in Task Manager during your heaviest game. If it stays well below its limit, your memory is fine, however high In use looks. If it gets close and the game stutters, close apps first. For what's holding memory on the desktop, see what is using my RAM.
