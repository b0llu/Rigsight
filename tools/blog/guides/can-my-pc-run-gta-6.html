GTA 6 comes out on 19 November on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. As of 18 November, Rockstar hasn't announced a PC version, a PC date or PC requirements. So the honest answer to "can my PC run GTA 6" is that nobody knows yet, including the sites with spec tables. What you can know is how your PC handles today's heaviest open-world games. Mine has 36 hours of Phantom Blade Zero on record, and that tells me more than any guessed list.

How RigSight caught it

RigSight records every play's frame rate from RivaTuner, next to how busy the GPU and CPU were, the graphics memory the game used and how full Windows' memory got. On the Games page, each game's Can it run better? card says how often the GPU was at full load, 95% busy or more, and what sets the pace when it isn't. Since 1.13.0, a game's page also has a Memory card beside Heat and power.

Here's Phantom Blade Zero on my RTX 3080 Ti and Ryzen 7 5700X3D, at my settings.

  • 68 FPS on average, with a 1% low of 50.
  • The GPU at full load in 87% of the minutes. It's the limit.
  • The CPU at 47% on average, never near full.
  • About 7 GB of graphics memory for the game itself, of the card's 12.
  • Windows' committed memory at 94% on average, back when the PC had 16 GB.

Crimson Desert looked much the same, at about 71 FPS with the GPU at full load 89% of the time. RigSight is free and open source.

What's known about GTA 6 on PC

  • Rockstar's site lists PS5 and Xbox Series X|S for the 19 November launch, and no PC version.
  • GamesBeat reported, from four anonymous sources, that the PC version is planned for early 2027. Rockstar hasn't confirmed it.
  • Take-Two has said news about a PC version will come from Rockstar.
  • Rockstar's last two big games came to PC later. GTA V took about 19 months after consoles, and Red Dead Redemption 2 about a year.

Why GTA 6 PC requirements lists are guesses

Every list you see is built from something else, GTA V's newer PC edition, the consoles' hardware, or other new games. The lists disagree with each other, and some sites call them leaks without saying where from. Rockstar published minimum and recommended specs for its earlier PC versions, and until it does for GTA 6, there's nothing real to check your PC against.

Can my PC run GTA 6? Check it with today's heaviest game

Play your heaviest open-world game for an hour, with an overlay or a tool that records, and look at four things.

  1. The frame rate and the 1% low. A 1% low far below the average means stutters you'll feel. 1% low FPS explains it.
  2. GPU usage. Near 100% means the GPU sets the pace, and lower settings or upscaling give more frames. Normal GPU usage while gaming has more.
  3. Graphics memory. A card already near full in today's games has the least room. How to check VRAM usage shows how.
  4. Committed memory in Task Manager. Near its limit, 16 GB is tight. Normal RAM usage while gaming covers it.

If your PC is comfortable in a big new open-world game today, it starts from a good place. If it's already at its limit, that's worth knowing before any big new game, and the real requirements will show how far off it is.

Should you upgrade for GTA 6 now?

Not for GTA 6 alone. Nobody knows yet what it needs on PC, and the PC version may be months away, with new parts and prices by then. If your PC runs out of something in the games you play today, that's a reason to upgrade now. What to upgrade in my PC first shows how to find which part.
