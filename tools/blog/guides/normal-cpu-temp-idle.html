RigSight has about 150 hours of minutes where my PC sat nearly idle, with the CPU and GPU both under 10% busy while I was at the desk. In those minutes my Ryzen 7 5700X3D averaged 48°C, and 9 in 10 of them stayed under 53°C. My RTX 3080 Ti averaged 48°C too, with most minutes between 45 and 50°C. That's a normal CPU temp at idle and a normal GPU one, and it's warmer than many people expect.

How RigSight caught it

RigSight records the CPU and GPU temperature every minute along with how busy each was, so it can pick out the minutes when the PC was resting. On the Temperatures page, the At rest, last 30 days card shows each chip's temperature at idle, today's next to its usual, and a line of the last 30 days. RigSight marks a chip that's warmer at rest than usual.

The resting temperature is more useful than the gaming one for spotting slow changes, because the load is the same every time. As the card puts it, a flat line means the cooling is holding, and a slow climb can come from dust or aging thermal paste, while a warmer room raises these too. CPU temperature needs RigSight to run with admin rights. RigSight is free and open source.

Normal CPU temp at idle

For most desktop CPUs, somewhere around 35 to 55°C at idle is normal. Where yours sits depends on a few things.

  • The CPU. Many AMD Ryzen chips idle in the 40s or 50s because they boost to high clocks for a moment even at light load. Intel chips often idle a little cooler.
  • The cooler. A big air cooler or a liquid cooler holds idle lower than the small cooler that came in the box.
  • The room. A room 5° warmer makes the idle about 5° warmer.
  • The fans. Quiet fan curves let the CPU sit warmer at idle on purpose.

My 5700X3D's idle minutes spread from the low 40s up to about 60°C. One in 10 was under 43°C, and only 1 in 100 reached 60.

Normal GPU temp at idle

Roughly 30 to 50°C. Many current cards stop their fans when cool and start them once the GPU passes a set point, often around 50 to 60°C. A card like that idles warmer, in the high 40s or low 50s, because nothing is moving air until it needs to. That's why my GPU's idle is so steady at 45 to 50°C. Its fans sit still below about 52°C. GPU fan not spinning has more on that.

Two screens, a high refresh rate or video playing can keep a GPU's memory clock up at idle, which adds a few degrees.

Why idle temperatures jump

Open a browser, and the CPU jumps 10 or 20° within a second, then falls back. Background tasks do the same thing on their own, like Windows Update, antivirus scans, indexing and app updaters. The CPU's temperature follows its boost almost instantly, so a tool that refreshes every second shows a jumpy line. What counts is where it settles. Normal CPU temperature while gaming covers the jumps under load.

When a warm idle is worth fixing

  • The CPU sits above about 60°C, or the GPU above about 60°C, with nothing running.
  • The idle temperature has climbed over weeks or months at the same room temperature.
  • Fans run loud at idle to hold the temperature.

If so, check Task Manager for an app keeping the CPU busy first. Then clean dust from the filters, the CPU cooler and the GPU, check that the case fans pull air in at the front and push it out at the back, and set Windows to the Balanced power plan. If it still idles hot on an older PC, new thermal paste on the CPU often helps.

Your next step is to note your idle temperatures today, with nothing running. That number is your own normal, and it's the one to compare against in a few months.
