Your phone tells you every week how long you looked at it. Your PC, where you might spend far more hours, says nothing. If you want to know your screen time on a Windows 11 or 10 PC, how long you spend in each app, or how much time you spend gaming, here is what Windows can show you and where it stops.

Does Windows have screen time?

Not for your own account. Windows has no screen time page for an adult using their own PC, on Windows 11 or Windows 10. What it has is a few tools built for other jobs that each show a part of the picture.

Microsoft Family Safety, for family members

Microsoft Family Safety is the closest thing Windows has to phone screen time. A family organizer can see which apps and games another member of the family group used, and for how long, by day and by week. It is built for parents keeping an eye on a child's account.

  1. Sign in at family.microsoft.com, or in the Microsoft Family Safety app, with the organizer's Microsoft account.
  2. Add the family member, who needs their own Microsoft account and signs in to Windows with it.
  3. Open the member's page and turn on Activity reporting.
  4. Open Screen time to see time per device, app and game.

Only family organizers can turn activity reporting on or off and see the reports. Idle time counts toward time limits but not toward the report, so the report can be lower than the limit suggests.

Set screen time limits on a PC

If what you want is to set screen time on a PC rather than check it, Family Safety does that too, again for members of a family group. The organizer can set screen time limits for Windows, Xbox and Android, and limits for single apps and games, each with a daily time and the hours it is allowed, the same every day or different per weekday. When time runs out, the member can ask for more, and the organizer decides. Time limits don't cover websites, which are blocked separately.

For yourself, Windows has no limit switch. The honest route is to measure first. Limits set from a guess tend to feel wrong, while a week of real numbers shows which app is worth limiting.

Battery usage on a laptop

On a laptop, open Settings > System > Power & battery and expand Battery usage. Pick Last 7 days and sort the apps by In use or Background. This shows how much battery each app used, not how long you used it. It only covers time on battery, and a desktop PC doesn't have this page at all.

Task Manager's App history

Task Manager has an App history tab. It shows CPU time and network use per app since a date, not how long the app was open or in front. An app can sit open all day and use almost no CPU time, so it is not a screen time figure.

How much time do I spend gaming?

Each launcher counts its own games. In Steam, your library shows the hours you have played on each game's page. Other launchers keep their own counts in different places, and some only show when you last played. None of them adds up all your launchers, or splits your gaming by day so you can see a week at a glance. If you play games from several launchers, which most people do, you have to add it up yourself.

What should count as screen time

Before you trust any number, it helps to know what it counted. A useful screen time figure counts time when an app was in front and you were actually at the PC. That leaves out an app open behind others, a PC left on overnight, and time asleep. The tricky case is a film or a game cutscene, where you don't touch the mouse or keyboard for a long time but are still watching. A good tool lets you decide whether full screen apps count.

Check your screen time with RigSight

RigSight, a free and open source PC monitor, records which app is in front, and whether you are at the PC, all day, and keeps it. It is the screen time page Windows doesn't have, for your own account.

Home has a Today so far card with your active time, how long the PC has been on, time away and your most used apps. The Apps page lists every app with its active time for a day, week, month, year, all time or a custom range. Open one to see its time in front, in the background and minimized, its longest session, how many sessions you had, and a chart of its active time across the period.

For gaming, Reports shows time in games beside your active time for any day, week, month or year, and Where the time went splits your time by kind of app, such as games, browsing and development. A strip shows your day minute by minute, coloured by the app in front. The Games page shows each game's total time played, whichever launcher it came from, how many times you played it and your longest play.

Reports also puts your usual beside each week and month, so you can see whether this week's gaming was more than normal for you, and Home's recap says plainly when a day ran longer or shorter than your usual for that weekday.

In Settings you choose after how many minutes without input you count as away, from 2 to 15 minutes, and whether full screen apps count as in use, so films and games are counted while you watch. Tracking can be paused for an hour or until you resume it. Your history stays on your PC.

If you want an honest number, start recording now and look at a full week before you decide anything. A single day says little. Write down your guess first, then compare it with the week's real hours per app and per game.
